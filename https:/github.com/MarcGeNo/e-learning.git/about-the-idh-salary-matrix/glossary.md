# Glossary

<table data-header-hidden><thead><tr><th width="274" valign="top"></th><th></th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td valign="top">Adjusted work year</td><td>Standard work year excluding paid annual leave and holidays and unpaid mandatory leave (52,143 * standard work week – hours of annual leave and holidays – hours of unpaid mandatory leave)</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Bonuses</td><td>An additional monetary payment, either fixed or variable, that a worker receives outside of their base wage.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Collective Bargaining agreement (CBA)</td><td>A written legal contract between an employer and a union representing the workers.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Gap per trade unit </td><td>Facility Annual Living Wage Gap / Number of production units.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Gender wage gap (GWG)</td><td>Difference between wages of women and men divided by men’s wages. It is a measure of wages inequality between employed men and women in the labor market.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Hours of mandatory unpaid leave</td><td>Number of hours of unpaid leave imposed upon the majority of the workers. This could occur if the facility is experiencing an emergency, such as a fire, or if the facility is closed due to flooding, maintenance, or other circumstances. It does not include weekly rest days. </td></tr><tr><td valign="top">In-kind benefits (IKB)</td><td>Non-monetary benefits such as food, transport, and housing that reduce the amount of cash income that workers need for a decent standard of living.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Job categories</td><td>Workers that do the same task, are paid in the same manner, and receive the same bonus and in-kind benefits. </td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Living wage (LW)</td><td>The wage level that is necessary to afford a decent standard of living for workers and their families, considering the country circumstances and calculated for the work performed during the normal hours of work. </td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Living Wage Contribution</td><td>Additional payment received intended to increase the welfare of workers when there is a gap between current and living wages. Voluntary contributions might come in the form of direct additional payments from clients but also as a premium or differential either allocated to wage increases or as an investment in community projects. </td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Living Wage Gap (LWG)</td><td>The difference between the actual compensation a worker receives and the income necessary to afford a decent standard of living in their specific location.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Non-time dependant bonus</td><td>Cash payment in addition to one’s normal rate the amount of which is fixed and does not depend on the number of days worked during the reporting year. These bonuses are usually paid annually or semi-annually, rather than continuously and are tied to time of the year, rather than performance. For example, a seasonal bonus. </td></tr><tr><td valign="top"><mark style="color:$primary;">Overtime</mark></td><td><mark style="color:$primary;">Working hours in addition to the standard workweek/regular working hours.</mark></td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Paid annual leave and holidays</td><td>Time away from work for which the employee receives compensation that workers are entitled to per year according to local labour laws or company policy. It does not include remunerated weekly rest days and personal leaves. </td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Paid personal leave</td><td>Paid time-off to cater for specific personal circumstances that workers are entitled to per year according to local labour laws or company policy (e.g. sick/disability leave, family care, bereavement leave, union representation days...).</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Piece rate work </td><td>Jobs in which payments (or wages) are made based on the number of units produced/worked during a specific period.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Remuneration (or compensation)</td><td>The total amount of wages, bonuses, and value of in-kind benefits granted to a worker in a specific period.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Smallholder</td><td>For the purpose of the Salary Matrix, a smallholder is defined as any farm that hires 10 or less full-time equivalent paid workers. "Full-time equivalent" is the person-time for one full-time job. For example, 4 part-time workers that each work 25% time is equal to 1 full-time equivalent worker. </td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Standard work year</td><td>Regular working hours per year (52.143 * standard workweek).</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Standard workweek</td><td>The set number of hours employees are expected to work in a regular workweek, or 48 hours whichever is lower.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Time-dependent bonus</td><td>Additional cash payments to the base wage depending on the number of hours/days worked during the reporting year. These bonuses can be variable incentives based on some measure of performance (for example, attendance bonuses, productivity bonuses and quality bonuses) or fixed allowances per day of work (e.g. transport allowance).</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Trade unit</td><td>A collection of goods or items that are treated as a single whole in commerce. This can include individual items, sets of items, or quantities that are recognized in the market as a single entity.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Unit of Production</td><td>“Unit of Production” refers to the units that are used to measure total production. For example, this could be pounds, kilograms, items, etc. </td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Voluntary unpaid license</td><td>Time away from work at the request of the worker that is not remunerated (e.g. absenteeism, personal days off…).</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Wage</td><td>Cash payment (or salary) received by a worker for a specific period, usually biweekly or monthly.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Work Area</td><td>Places where different jobs take place, such as an agricultural field, a packaging area, a manufacturing floor and office, or a certain station within a factory. Multiple workers or job categories can be part of one work area.</td></tr><tr><td valign="top">Worker Organization</td><td>Any organization established to represent the interests of workers, including recognized unions.</td></tr></tbody></table>
