# The IDH Salary Matrix

The [IDH Salary Matrix](https://www.salarymatrix.idhtrade.org/login) is a practical tool to help you evaluate how the total remuneration received by your employees (including wages, bonuses and in-kind benefits) compares to the relevant living wage estimates for your region. Hence, the tool supports supply chain efforts on wage transparency and shared responsibility for addressing living wage gaps.

If you are looking to assess living wages in your region, you can create an account and start using the tool for free here: [https://salarymatrix.idhtrade.org/](https://salarymatrix.idhtrade.org/)

<summary>The history of the tool</summary>

The Salary Matrix tool was originally developed by IDH and the Rainforest Alliance as an Excel file to measure potential gaps between workers’ total remuneration packages and living wage estimates. This Version 1 of the tool was published in December 2018 as part of the project ‘Next Steps in Sustainability: Measuring Impact and Assessing Living Wages’ in Costa Rica and Belize, based on findings from seven pilot farms that volunteered to better understand the value of their salaries, bonuses, and benefits.

In May 2019, IDH held a public consultation to further refine the tool. The valuable feedback from private sector partners, certification bodies, and others laid the foundation for improvements to help all supply chain actors take effective action towards living wages. With support from Fairtrade, Rainforest Alliance, and others, further pilot tests then took place between September 2019 and January 2020 — collecting data from the tea, banana, coffee, pineapple, and flower sector in eight countries with the new digital platform. All feedback was reviewed by the [Roadmap on Living Wages](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/living-wage-platform/), an initiative launched by IDH in 2019 to enable companies to work on the definitions, tools, and approaches to living wages in a uniformed way.

Since then, IDH has been leading supporting partners engaged in the development of the digital tool — from organizing consultation meetings to compiling feedback, analysing recommendations, and identifying practical ways to incorporate them into an improved second version of the Salary Matrix.

As a result of these efforts, in spring 2020, the Road map members agreed on improvements to the Salary Matrix, which resulted in the publication of Version 2. In 2022, a new system for wage data entry was implemented and the option to upload data via Excel included. In 2023, improved calculations formulas and the option to capture living wage contributions were added.

Responding to the user request to simplify data entering and to facilitate the escalation on the use of the tool, the current Version 3 was released in January 2026, and after a period of consultation meetings, feedback analysis and tool testing, including the below improvements:

* A cleaner interface: easier to navigate and quicker to get the work done in a way that ensures that information and questions are relevant to the user.
* Simplified workflows and methodology: streamlined data entry process and calculation methodology, cutting out unnecessary steps and complexity to reduce effort and improve reliability.
* Full transparency: new dedicated section where the math behind the results is fully explained.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Why should I use this tool?</summary>

IDH believes that the only way prevailing wages can be measured accurately is by using tools like the Salary Matrix and that is why we launched it. Through this tool, companies can truly get a sense of what their own situation is vis-à-vis a living wage estimate in the regions they produce.

For certain sectors in certain countries, prevailing wage data may be available. While this may be useful to have a preliminary understanding of wages for the sector, it cannot be used in place of the Salary Matrix to compare wages against living wage for several reasons:

* Prevailing wage information is often season specific, and it does not fully capture what workers are earning during the full year of production.
* Also, it often focuses on one job category, such as the lowest paid workers, and does not include all workers in a facility.
* Most importantly, prevailing wage data often does not specify hours worked or adjust to the standard work week which is essential for comparing against living wage estimates.
* Prevailing wage data is also general, and not specific to a facility which may pay more or less than sector or country prevailing wages.

Wage information from Collective Bargaining Agreements (CBAs) may also be available and informative. However, it does not include sufficient detail to understand workers’ actual take home pay or value of received in-kind benefits.

The Salary Matrix closely follows and builds on the Anker methodology® on calculating current wages to compare with living wage estimates.

Specifications for the Salary Matrix methodology were consulted with technical experts as part of the Technical Advisory Group. The tool has also been used by hundreds of producers and feedback has been and continues to be incorporated to improve the methodology.

Through this tool, companies can truly get a sense of what their own situation is vis-à- vis the living wage estimates in the regions they produce.

The Salary Matrix has proven to be useful to:

* Calculate current remuneration including bonuses and in-kind benefits.
* Comparing against living wage estimates.
* Unveiling barriers and finding solutions together with suppliers and retailers.
* Tracking yearly progress over time.
* Supporting work with certification programs that introduce requirements for living wages.
* Beginning to understand gendered aspects of work.
* Raising awareness in the entire value chain.
* And more!

</details>

<details>

<summary>Who can use this tool?</summary>

The Salary Matrix can be used by all types of organizations from any sector and any size.

However, for the agricultural sector, IDH recommends the Living Income approach for smallholders (less than 10 full time employees).

IDH makes this recommendation to strike a balance between feasibility and impact. It is the goal that the Salary Matrix be available to as many farms as possible to support farms' negotiating power with buyers, while reaching as many workers as possible.&#x20;

If a standard/certification system defines a smallholder as having fewer workers, then the user should use the definition provided by their standard/certification program. If the standard/certification program defines a smallholder as having a greater number of workers, then it is up to the user to decide which definition they prefer to use.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What is the cost of using this tool?</summary>

The IDH Salary Matrix is completely free to use. There is no cost associated with accessing the tool, entering data, or generating reports.

The only costs you could find are:

* If you were to purchase a living wage estimate, if there is not a free one available in the region you want to conduct your research.
* Training costs for the use of the tool: if you are interested in receiving information about the training costs, please send a request for information to: livingwagematrix@idhtrade.org.

</details>
