# The Methodology

<details>

<summary>What is the definition of a Living Wage?</summary>

According to the ILO, a living wage is:

> _“The wage level that is necessary to afford a decent standard of living for workers and their families, taking into account the country circumstances and calculated for the work performed during the normal hours of work;_
>
> _This includes food, housing, health and education, and other necessary goods and services, in accordance with national circumstances. “_

</details>

<details>

<summary>How are living wages calculated?</summary>

The living wage is estimated by first establishing the cost for the basic but decent life in a specific region, inclusive of food, housing, education, healthcare, transportation, care and other essential needs for a family including unexpected events. The exact costs reflect local items and prices.

The total costs for a family are then divided by the typical number of working adults to reflect other wage earners contribution to the family earnings. This value indicates the necessary take home pay for one wage earner, or the net living wage, in a specific region for a single month. Finally, this net living wage is adjusted to consider taxes and deductions.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Who calculates living wage estimates?</summary>

Estimates are calculated by independent researchers and organizations. Each methodology has its own approach for estimating living wages.&#x20;

To support companies and organisations in identifying rigorous and consistent methodologies, IDH has developed a process to recognise living wage estimate methodologies that meet [twelve specific criteria](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/uploaded/2025/06/IDH_Recognition_Criteria_7.9_EN.pdf).

The recognition criteria are objective criteria for the minimum elements a living wage methodology needs to include to be recognised by IDH.

The below methodologies have been recognised so far:

* [Full-fledged Anker Methodology](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/uploaded/2025/06/Living-Wage-Benchmark-Series_Anker_NEW.pdf)
* [WageIndicator Typical Family Methodology](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/uploaded/2025/06/Living-Wage-Benchmark-Series_Wage-Indicator-Typical-Family_new.pdf)
* [Fair Wage Network Typical Family Methodology](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/uploaded/2025/06/Living-Wage-Benchmark-Series_Fair-Wage-Network-Typical-Family_NEW-1.pdf)
* [Anker Reference Value Methodology](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/uploaded/2026/02/IDH_ARI-Ref-Value_Recommended-LWEM_FINAL.docx)
* [Living Wage for US Monthly Methodology](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/uploaded/2025/06/Living-Wage-Benchmark-Series_Living-Wage-for-Us_NEW.pdf)

To know if a recognised methodology has an estimate for your region of interest, search on our [**Living Wage Identifier Tool**](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/living-wage-identifier-tool/).

When available for your region and year of interest, the Roadmap on Living Wages recommends the use of estimates developed using the full-fledged Anker methodology. If not available, another IDH-recognised Methodology may be selected.

To read more about living wages estimates see the [Step 1](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/living-wage-platform/what-is-a-living-wage/) of the living wage roadmap.

</details>

<details>

<summary>How is the comparable remuneration calculated?</summary>

To be compared with a living wage estimate, the remuneration shall be calculated following the same methodology.

The remuneration, for comparison with the living wage, has three components:

* **Wages**: monetary payment corresponding to a specific period of work, usually every fourteen days or monthly.
* **Bonuses**: additional monetary payment, fixed or variable, that the worker receives.
* **In-kind benefits**: non-monetary benefit (product or service) provided to the worker that reduces the amount of monetary income the worker needs to afford a decent standard of living

<details>

<summary>What is a Living wage gap?</summary>

The living wage gap is how far away workers are from earning a living wage. This means comparing the living wage estimate to the current remuneration, in each facility.

![](<../.gitbook/assets/Unknown image (3)>)

IDH has developed the **Salary Matrix**to calculate the current remuneration (including wages, bonuses, and in-kind benefits). Once remuneration is calculated, the tool compares it with the living wage estimate to provide insights into the living wage gaps.

</details>
