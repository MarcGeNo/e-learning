# Entering In-kind benefits

### Eligibility

{% hint style="info" %}
In-kind benefits should not be considered as partial payment of wages if the country’s legislation forbids it.
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
Enter values per worker per year adjusted to full-time equivalent.
{% endhint %}

When allowed by national or local legislation, only certain in-kind benefits are counted toward a living wage. To be eligible, these benefits must:

* be available to all workers in the job category,
* be accepted and valued by workers,
* meet safety and quality standards,
* be additional to what is freely available through public systems,
* adhere to the elements of a living wage per the living wage definition and
* cover a basic cost of living as identified in the living wage estimate.

The principles guiding the Salary Matrix are drawn from the Anker Methodology. More information on how the Anker Methodology approaches in-kind benefits can be found [here](https://www.elgaronline.com/monochap-oa/9781786431455/chapter16.xhtml).

Eligible in-kind benefits include:

* **Child education support**: these are benefits that reduce workers' costs for children’s primary and secondary education, such as tuition or school supplies or transportation. Must be additional to what is provided for free by the public services. If funding is provided directly to the school, the total amount should be divided per total number of students at the school, and then this per-student amount may be included for every child of a worker who attends such school.
* **Care support**: This is the cost to employer for providing childcare for workers' children and/or elder care services that reduce workers' care costs, such as free childcare or stipends for child or elder care services. Not all living wage estimation methodologies recognised by IDH include care as a cost of a decent living, therefore care in-kind benefits can only be included if care cost are included in the living wage estimate.
* **Food**: These benefits include free or subsidized meals regularly taken by the majority of workers. Only full meals can be included, and they must be regularly provided. Ideally, meals provided should be balanced and follow local or international nutrition guidance. The value of snacks and water are not included. Given farm produces shall not be included unless the amount delivered and the cost is agreed in the CBA and valued by workers
* **Transportation**: These benefits replace or reduce the transportation costs that workers would otherwise pay for themselves. Transport must meet local safety standards and transport workers to and from work or to and from town on weekends. For example, transportation costs could include free transportation to work, free transportation for all needs in the town of residence, and transportation passes.
* **Healthcare**: These benefits reduce the cost to workers for their and/or their families’ healthcare needs. Examples of qualifying healthcare benefits include free medical services that are not otherwise freely available through public systems, or stipends to cover the portion of workers’ costs to access public healthcare systems. Qualifying healthcare benefits do not include the provision of poorly managed clinics, or clinics that are only available for occupational health or first aid services; or the employer’s portion of legally mandated national health insurance. Healthcare costs are not counted if the country provides for free the same healthcare services which are widely regarded as decent in terms of quality and availability.
* **Housing**: Housing benefits are only counted if:
  * The housing provided must be single-family housing for a worker and her/his family (dormitory-style housing would not count toward a living wage). Single workers who do not have a spouse or children are considered a family of 1; in this case, multi-family housing may be included if it meets decency and privacy standards (no shared bedrooms).
  * The housing meets quality requirements that are either detailed in the living wage estimate or (when an estimate does not specify) meets other international housing standards such as those detailed in applicable sustainability standards.

#### Cost allocation

To value in-kind benefits, the facility shall calculate the total cost to the employer and divide this by the total number of employees in the facility.

For in-kind benefits with different values (e.g. houses of different sizes or school support depending on the number of children), use the average: cost to the employer / number of workers who can qualify for the in-kind benefit.

The value of each in-kind benefit shall not exceed any of the following:

* Its replacement cost if purchased by the employee; or
* The cost of an equivalent home-prepared meal in cases of provided food; or
* The amount allocated for the item in the living wage estimate.

If an in-kind benefit requires that the employee share some of the cost, the employee’s cost should be subtracted from the total value.

{% hint style="info" %}
For workers that did not work all year-round, please enter the value of in-kind benefits that these workers would have received if they had worked the entire year.
{% endhint %}

#### Caps

Based on the principles for in-kind benefits outlined in the Anker MethodologyÒ, the Salary Matrix caps the contribution of in-kind benefits to ensure the worker right to self-determination.

The combined total value of in-kind benefits is capped at 30% of total remuneration. Housing is capped at 15% of total remuneration. All other benefits are capped at 10% of total remuneration.

Estimating maximum value for each in kind benefit is not as simple as it might seem because rules on maximum percentage allowed refer to the total wage received, which includes the value of in-kind benefits.

However, the Salary Matrix makes this very easy. Simply enter the yearly cost per worker to the employer of the in-kind benefits and the Salary Matrix will adjust them automatically, using the simplified guidance provided by the Anker’s:

> _1. Use 11.1% (1/.9−1) of cash wages for each allowed in kind benefit besides housing (and 17.6% for housing) when there is only one acceptable in-kind benefit._

> _2. Use 12.7% of cash wages for each allowed in kind benefit besides housing when there are two or more acceptable in kind benefits (i.e. average of 11.1% allowed when one in kind benefit and maximum of 14.3% allowed when the total value of all in kind benefits equals or exceeds the 30% of wages limit), and 19.5% (average of 17.6% and 21.3%) of cash wage for housing._

> _3. Apply a final check to steps 1 and 2 by adding up the values of all in kind benefits and reduce this total to a maximum of 42.9% of cash wages when the total exceeds 42.9% of cash wages._

{% hint style="info" %}
On the other hand, the total value for all in-kind benefits as partial payment for wages should not bring the cash wage below the minimum wage. Please make sure that this is the case since the Salary Matrix does not take into consideration this limitation.
{% endhint %}
