# Add-ons

The next page allows to enter additional information to your final report, depending on the purpose of the report. You can select both options or any.

![](<../../.gitbook/assets/Unknown image (32)>)

If you select an **Audit report**, you will be required to enter information on worker representation and pice-rate workers that will help auditors to better prepare for the verification.

If you select **Gap per trade unit** report you will be required to enter some Product information to allow for the calculation of the Gap per trade unit.

<details>

<summary><strong>Living wage contributions</strong></summary>

Living wage contributions are additional payments you may receive for your products, that are intended to increase the welfare of workers when there is a gap between current and living wages.

Contributions might come in the form of direct additional payments from your clients but also as a premium or differential either allocated to wage increases or as an investment in community projects.

{% hint style="info" %}
&#x20;**Contributions shall not be included as remuneration because they are not guaranteed payments.**
{% endhint %}

While these contributions could temporarily alleviate living wage gaps, the ultimate objective is for workers to earn living wages that are properly negotiated through social dialogue and collective bargaining agreements. Therefore, the Salary Matrix allows you to capture information on voluntary contributions in this section, separately from payroll.&#x20;

Registering received living wage contributions will let you know the % of the total facility gap covered by the contributions.

Including the contributions information is not a requirement but will help you track the total amount of external contributions that were distributed to workers (whether in cash or vouchers) in a given year. These amounts will be visually represented in your report (against your facility’s cumulative living wage gap), showcasing how such contributions are helping you temporarily address living wage gaps.

{% hint style="info" %}
Since contributions do not have an impact on the living wage gap calculations, IDH does not provide specific guidelines on how and when contributions should be registered. Please refer to your living wage gap closing program to reach a consensus on registering contributions under "Add ons".
{% endhint %}

**How to enter the Contributions**

When you answer Yes to the questions "Received Living Wage Controbutions?"  the below  menu will display:

<figure><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/Captura de pantalla 2026-05-28 a les 9.32.43.png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

* **Provider of contribution**: Here you can share the name of the party from whom the contribution was received, for example "Retailer ABC". This entry will be shown on the report table as a contributor. In case you received multiple 'types' of contribution from the same party, you can name them as a contributor more than once, with different types selected from the drop down menu for each relevant amount.
* **Type of contribution**: here you can choose among different types of contributions.
* **Total amount of contribition received**: This is the total amount that was received from the contributor under a certain contribution type. The currency follows that of the  Calculation. If the currency of the contribution was in a different one than the one used in the rest of the Calculation, please convert it into the currency used for the wages in the Calculation. You can utilize the forex rates prevailing at the time of entering this field.
* **Amount paid to the workers in cas**h: This includes cash and bank transfers received directly from the employer or through any other sources such as accounts managed by worker representatives.
* **Amount paid to the workers in vouchers**: vouchers can be only counted in this field if:
  * &#x20;they are aligned with the way workers would like to receive such contributions aimed at alleviating living wage gaps and&#x20;
  * provided in coordination with unions or agreed under collective bargaining agreements.

{% hint style="info" %}
Note that the registration of contributions paid as in-kind benefits and the allocation of contributions to specific workers or job categories are not yet available.
{% endhint %}

If you have received more that one contribution form different clients, you can add additional contributions by clicking on the “Add contribution +” button.

<details>

<summary><strong>Indirect contracting of workers via third party.</strong></summary>

Labour providers/intermediaries can be temporary work agencies, recruitment agencies, recruiters, labour brokers, labour agents, gangmasters...

The employment contract might either be with the 3rd party (e.g. a temporary work agency hires and pays the worker and charges a daily fee to the facility) or with the facility (e.g. an agent refers workers to the facility which hires and pays the workers, while worker’s pay an employment fee to the agent). The worker is supervised by the facility.

Service providers: in this case the contract is not an employment contract but a provision of services agreement (e.g. security or canteen services). Employees might be self-employed or hired, paid and supervised by a service provider.

</details>
